# Bringing IRC (and The Lounge) to the 21st century

TheLounge v4.6.0

The Lounge 4.6.0 was just released and I am excited. It brings many new features and improvements on both the user-facing client side and in the technical sense.

For those unfamiliar, The Lounge is a self-hosted IRC client. You host it on an always on server, and it acts as an always-online bouncer so your messages are preserved even if no web client is connected. We also support a public mode for public usage without database message persistence.

On the internal side of things, our last few releases replaced Webpack and Babel with Vite as our bundler and build system, and we're now on Vite 8 with Rolldown. Local development and HMR got noticeably faster, and production
bundle sizes were halved thanks to better tree-shaking and bundling.

The real exciting news is in the new features. IRCv3 support was a focus of mine this release and we are now one of the most supporting clients in the client registry.

The new features include:

</details>

- MONITOR / extended-monitor (away status)
- emoji reactions (`+draft/react`)
- reply threading (`+draft/reply`)
- typing indicators (`+typing`)
- multiline (`draft/multiline`)
- Bot Mode

Let's look into each. Most of these are server-side extensions and need a compatible IRCd. The client-only tags (those prefixed with `+`) are relayed by the server but interpreted by clients.


### MONITOR / extended-monitor support

Traditionally, IRC clients track user status — like away messages — by periodically polling the server with `WHO`/`WHOIS`. The `MONITOR` and `extended-monitor` specs turn this into an event-driven system: a client subscribes once and receives away (and account) updates as they happen. That lets us ship live status indicators like away dots without the constant `WHO`
polling older clients rely on (and that would flood IRC servers)

 ### Emoji Reactions

For supporting servers, The Lounge users can now react to messages with emojis. This is possible in a new on-hover/tap menu that is also used for reply threading.

![Screenshot 2026-07-19 at 17-26-37 #ergo — The Lounge](</Users/max/Downloads/Screenshot 2026-07-19 at 17-26-37 #ergo — The Lounge.png>)

 ### Reply Threading

Familiar to those who have used Discord or Slack, you can now also reply to specific messages. Replies should work with all clients that support the message-id spec, and non-supporting clients will fallback gracefully to normal messages

![Screenshot 2026-07-19 at 17-27-26 #thelounge — The Lounge](</Users/max/Downloads/Screenshot 2026-07-19 at 17-27-26 #thelounge — The Lounge.png>)

 ### Typing Indicators

Typing indicator support was one of our [most upvoted issues](https://github.com/thelounge/thelounge/issues/2419) and I'm very happy to see it finally ship.

![image](/Users/max/Downloads/image.png)

By default, the setting is set to "receive", meaning you will see other's typing notification but you will not send any of your own.

 ### And more

We also shipped a handful of other IRCv3 features: [Bot Mode](https://ircv3.net/specs/extensions/bot-mode),
[multiline](https://ircv3.net/specs/extensions/multiline), standard-replies, and channel-context.

Multiline lets a single logical message span multiple lines instead of being split into separate ones. standard-replies gives us structured `FAIL`/`WARN`/`NOTE` responses from the server, and channel-context (`+draft/channel-context`) lets
messages carry the channel they relate to.

Bot Mode is self-explanatory but more important than ever in this world of [talking weights](/blog/weights). It's simple enough: bots set a flag in their user mode, and clients are now notified — so we badge them with a `[bot]` indicator in the userlist.
